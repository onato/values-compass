 Graduate Nurse Job Guarantee

 Labour will offer every eligible New Zealand-trained graduate nurse a job in our health system.

 Key facts

 Every eligible New Zealand-trained graduate nurse will be offered a job in the health system

 Around 2,400 nurses graduates every year will get a job offer

 Roles will be offered on graduation rather than staggered throughout the year, starting in 2027, and will be at least 0.8 FTE

 Graduate nurses will be offered jobs through the existing Advanced Choice of Employment (ACE) and primary care employment schemes

 Registered and enrolled nurses are eligible

 Double funding for graduate nursing roles in primary and community healthcare

 Costed at $525 million. Funded from the Budget's Vote Health cost-pressure funding

If you train as a nurse here and qualify, there will be a place for you here under a Labour Government.

 How it works

 Every eligible New Zealand-trained graduate nurse will be offered a role through the existing Advanced Choice of Employment (ACE) and primary care employment schemes.

 Jobs will be offered at graduation, rather than staggered throughout the year, and will be for at least 0.8 full-time equivalent (FTE).

 The policy means all of the estimated 2,400 nurses who graduate a year will get a job offer. Both registered and enrolled nurses are eligible.

 Labour will double funding for graduate nursing roles in primary and community healthcare.

 Why it matters

 New Zealand needs more nurses in our hospitals, primary care, aged care, community services and rural areas.

 But under National, we're training nurses here only for too many of them to find there's no job for them after they graduate.

 National has made it harder to hire the nurses our hospitals need. They've had a hiring freeze in place, and in two years, just 54 nurses have been added to the public health system.

 In 2025, fewer than half of graduating nurses were initially offered jobs through the ACE programme. Some waited months for an offer, while others were offered less than 0.8 FTE.

 When hospitals can't hire the nurses they need, there are fewer nurses on the ward, more pressure on the staff who are there, and patients wait longer for care.

 Nurses who have been trained in New Zealand, and want to work in our communities, have no other option but to look overseas for work.

 We can't afford another three years of National holding our health system back.

 A smart investment

 Backing graduate nurses means more nurses caring for patients, less pressure on existing staff, and a better start for people beginning their careers.

 The Graduate Nurse Job Guarantee means all of the estimated 2,400 nurses who graduate a year will get a job offer.

 It will apply to graduates sitting their State Final examination from July 2026 onwards. From 2027, eligible graduates will be offered roles on graduation.

 Eligibility and cost

 Job offers will depend on graduates passing their State Final examination and required pre-employment checks.

 Graduates must also be a New Zealand citizen, permanent resident, returning resident visa holder or Australian citizen, and have completed their nursing qualification at a New Zealand tertiary institution.

 The cost of the Graduate Nurse Job Guarantee is $525 million. It will be funded from health cost-pressure funding.

 Nursing numbers under National, by district

 Under National, recruitment has been restricted across the country. In some areas, including Auckland and Counties Manukau, the number of nurses has actually fallen. Jobs under the Graduate Nurse Job Guarantee will be available across New Zealand, in hospitals, primary care and community healthcare, helping fill these gaps.

 Frequently asked questions

 How many nurses will this add?

 Around 2,400 nurses graduate in New Zealand every year, and Labour will ensure every eligible graduate gets a job offer. Both registered and enrolled nurses are eligible.

 When will the guarantee apply from?

 It will apply to graduates sitting their State Final examination from July 2026 onwards. Eligible graduates will be offered a job in a hospital, primary care or community healthcare setting. From 2027, roles will be offered on graduation rather than staggered throughout the year.

 Who is eligible?

 The policy applies to graduates who sit their State Final examination from July 2026 onwards and complete a pre-registration nursing qualification approved by the Nursing Council of New Zealand. 

 Job offers will depend on graduates passing their State Final examination and all required pre-employment checks. They must also:

 Be a New Zealand citizen, New Zealand permanent resident or returning resident visa holder, or an Australian citizen

 Have studied their qualification in New Zealand at a New Zealand tertiary institution

 Why are you doing this?

 New Zealand needs more nurses. But right now, we are training nurses here only for too many of them to find there is no job for them after they graduate.

 National has made it harder to hire the nurses our hospitals need. They've had a hiring freeze in place, and in two years, just 54 nurses have been added to the public health system.

 In 2025, fewer than half of graduating nurses were initially offered jobs through the ACE programme. Some waited months for an offer, while others were offered less than 0.8 FTE. That leaves graduate nurses out of work or looking overseas at the same time our health system needs them.

 Labour will back our graduate nurses to find a job here at home.

 Where will the jobs be?

 Jobs will be available across New Zealand in hospitals, primary care and community healthcare. Under National, recruitment has been restricted across the country - in some areas, including Auckland and Counties Manukau, the number of nurses has actually fallen. Labour will make sure graduate nurses can move from training into work, while helping fill the gaps across our health system.

 What will it cost and how will you pay for it?

 The Graduate Nurse Job Guarantee will cost $525 million. It will be funded from Vote Health cost-pressure funding.

Hunters and Firearms

Protect Lawful Hunting

Hunters acting safely and within the law can be deliberately obstructed by people who step into the line of fire to stop a shot. That creates an obvious danger, but there is no specific infringement offence for it.
Aircraft can also be used to harass game-bird shooters by deliberately scattering or flushing birds. This disturbs the birds and sabotages a lawful activity, with little legal remedy available.
People remain free to oppose hunting. They should not be free to interfere in ways that put others at risk.
Read the full policy here.
ACT will:
Create an infringement offence for deliberately standing in the line of fire of a lawful hunter.

Create an infringement offence for using drones or helicopters to harass game-bird shooters.

In our team's words:
ACT Deputy Leader Nicole McKee says:
“A hunter acting safely and lawfully should not have someone step into the line of fire to stop a shot. That is not ordinary protest. It creates an immediate risk for everyone there, yet the law has no specific answer.
“Hunters and game-bird shooters are expected to follow strict safety rules, as they should. Those rules cannot work if someone deliberately enters the line of fire. The law needs a clear consequence for that behaviour.”
ACT Conservation spokesperson Cameron Luxton says:
“The same principle applies when activists use drones or helicopters to scatter game birds. It disturbs the birds, disrupts a lawful shoot and can create another hazard in the air and on the ground.
“We would create a specific infringement offence for harassing game-bird shooters with an aircraft. If someone wants to campaign against hunting, they remain free to do so. They should not be free to sabotage a lawful activity in a way that puts people at risk.”

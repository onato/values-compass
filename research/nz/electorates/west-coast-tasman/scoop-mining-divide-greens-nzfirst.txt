 The Mining Political Divide: Greens Vs NZ First

Wednesday, 19 August 2026, 6:14 pm

 Article: Vihan Dalal - Local Democracy Reporter

 The Green Party and NZ First have shown to be poles apart
with their mining policies after a grilling from the West
Coast Regional Council members.
Councillors have been
putting all candidates in the West Coast-Tasman electorate
on the mat, giving each time to outline their policies and
answer questions.
The Greens and NZ First had their
turn on Tuesday.
Green Party candidate Andrew Beaumont
defended his party's stance on mining, saying they were
against mining "in the wrong place, in the wrong way", while
NZ First candidate Jamie Cleine says mining royalties for
the West Coast is a priority.
The West Coast is in the
midst of a mining boom, including the $400 million Snowy
River underground goldmine which expects to be hauling out
gold by the end of this year.
The mine expects to grow
its workforce from 90 to 200 over the next 12
months.
Bathurst's Stockton opencast coalmine is
Westport's largest employer, and the company is currently
looking to expand into the adjoining Denniston Plateau via a
fast-track application.
That plan has met with
strident opposition from Forest and Bird, which gathered
30,000 signatures, mainly online, for a petition presented
to Parliament earlier this month.
Cr Andy Campbell
opened the questions yesterday [Tuesday] and said the Green
Party was known for its anti-mining stance and asked Mr
Beaumont if that that would still be the case if the Greens
were part of any future government.
Mr Beaumont said
they were not anti-mining.

"The Green Party is not
against mining, per se. They are against mining done in the
wrong place in the wrong way.
"And we recognise that
this is a finite supply. If we want to take it all now and
some people make a lot of money, that is great for them, but
what about the generations to come?"
He said the West
Coast would have to move away from extractive industries and
look at other ways of creating jobs, though he admitted that
cannot be achieved quickly.
Coalmines were closing
down because they were "not economic", Mr Beaumont said,
citing India reducing its dependence on coal.
"India
is trying to reduce its coal imports and reduce the price
that it pays, and the price that we are asking for in New
Zealand, they do not want to pay that any more."
He
advocated for a "just transition" away from mining,
including financial support to communities to develop
industries.
"The concept of just transition is
financial support from central government to develop
industry so that people retain their jobs and it is done by
working with communities to work from one position to
another.
"It is not to say, 'this must stop now'
because that does not work."
When asked by Cr Mark
McIntyre about his thoughts on coalmining, Mr Beaumont said
relying on mining companies put the community at
risk.
"The problem with big mining companies is if you
put all of your eggs in a basket with the big mining
company, you are putting your community at risk by relying
on international market pressure and their own market
pressure.
"Companies like that have no loyalty to
anything but their profits and their shareholders and if
things change, which they are changing fast across the
world, at any point it could stop," Mr Beaumont
said.
NZ First's Jamie Cleine, until last year the
Mayor of Buller, told councillors the mining royalties
programme was central to his party's policy, which includes
returning 50% of mining royalties to the
region.
"Those funds can be invested in infrastructure
that delivers intergenerational benefit.
"It is trying
to solve that challenge we always get from those who are
anti-mining, that mining does not benefit communities
long-term, and it gets rid of that boom-bust
cycle."
Mr Cleine said the distribution of minerals
royalties would depend on the West Coast's
priorities.
"The mechanisms are obviously up for
debate. What is not up for debate is that 50% of the
royalties will be going back to the region."
Changes
in mining policy would include the creation of economic
zones where mining will be consented more quickly, as well
as geological surveys.
"Included in that policy is
better planning and some economic zones to enable mining to
be much more quickly consented. That suite also includes
geological surveys, understanding what we actually have
across New Zealand in terms of our mineral
resources.
"It looks to value add, so move away from
just a dig and ship model into more of processing
onshore."
NZ First would also invest in training and
development of the workforce, including a return to schools
of mines, along with training for high paying jobs on the
West Coast, he said.

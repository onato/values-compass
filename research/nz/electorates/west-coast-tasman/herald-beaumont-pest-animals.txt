The Green Party’s West Coast-Tasman candidate sidestepped questions on whether the party would back an idea of harvesting pest animals such as goats and deer.
At a candidate briefing before the West Coast Regional Council on Tuesday, candidate Andrew Beaumont said his party would “follow evidence” for the idea.

 It
 followed a recent suggestion by Hari Hari farmer and regional councillor Andy Campbell to turn pest animals into a food resource.

Campbell has previously told LDR that pest animals should be harvested for food instead of letting them rot on the forest floor.
He raised the suggestion as the council considers including pest animals into its next Regional Pest Management Plan.
Campbell had said wild goats should be harvested for food instead of importing them to New Zealand, describing it as the “most organic meat in the world”.
Deer numbers have increased on the West Coast and have been involved in collisions on both local roads and state highways.
Asked about this, Beaumont said the Green Party was firm in its stance.

“We mentioned ground-trapping as alternatives, but one of the things that we are really clear about in the Green Party is that we will do things evidence-based.
“We are not going to go off and go ‘we think this is the right thing to do because this is what we have done’.”
The Greens would work with local councils to address predator control issues, he said.
“The local people are the ones who know best.
“We are going to look at their input, we are going to look at the evidence-based input.”

Regional council chairman Colin Smith asked specifically if the Greens would support harvesting if it had economic benefits, including establishing foreign markets for them.
Beaumont repeated that they would work with evidence.
“Answering your question again, everything we look at is always evidence-based.
“We are going to look at all the evidence involved, and we are going to look at the information involved, so we are not closed to any options.”
Smith pressed Beaumont further and asked whether the party saw an economic opportunity in the use of pest animals:

“If an operator on the West Coast has sourced a market and there is regulation or there is policy that would in any way make it difficult for that operator to export that product to a foreign market, does the Green Party see an economic opportunity in the use of pest species?”
Beaumont repeated: “If something is presented to us and is viable, it is not going to badly impact the environment, it is going to provide an economic return, and all the evidence says that this works, then that is what we are going to support.”
All candidates in the West Coast-Tasman electorate have been invited to front the regional council forum in the lead-up to the election.

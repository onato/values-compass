The source catalogue

Independent, non-advocacy sources a research agent can trawl for evidence of how New Zealand parties and candidates align with the ten value dimensions. Supplies the concrete source hierarchy for step 1.3 of the generated research prompt. Version 1.1, compiled 2026-09-05, reviewed 2026-09-05. The full catalogue with access notes and licences is data/sources-nz.json.

Neutrality standard

Included: Official government, parliamentary, electoral-commission and national-statistics records; Peer-reviewed academic party-position datasets; Non-partisan civic tech that republishes official records without editorialising; Party-published manifestos, policy pages and costings, admitted only as evidence of that party's own stated positions, never as evidence about another party.

Excluded: Advocacy-group scorecards and ratings; Mission-driven think tanks; Commercial aggregators and opinion media.

Inclusion is judged on the source's relationship to the record, not on whether its findings are congenial. Sources that apply an editorial or simplifying layer are marked editorialised:true and should be treated as leads, not evidence.

Treasury fiscal documents (BPS, BEFU, FSR, HYEFU, PREFU) official
fiscal		 What a government actually spent and prioritised, versus what it said it would.
Public Finance Act s 26I requires every Fiscal Strategy Report to assess fiscal performance in the period since the most recent substantial change in the political party composition of the administration. That is a statutorily mandated, explicitly party-attributed, annually published performance assessment. The statutory cycle (BPS by 31 March, BEFU+FSR with the Budget, HYEFU 1 Nov-31 Dec, PREFU in election years) is dated and comparable, so a missing document is itself a finding. Context sources (not evidence of values)

  • Electoral Commission: how MMP works: The 5% party-vote threshold and the one-electorate-seat alternative, Sainte-Laguë allocation, overhang seats, and the distinction between the party vote and the electorate vote. Authoritative basis for telling the person what each of their two votes can and cannot change. URL UNVERIFIED.
  • Research Association New Zealand Political Polling Code: The disclosure standard a published poll should meet: sponsor, fieldwork dates, sample, method, weighting and margin of error. Use it to decide which polls to admit and what to demand of each citation. Membership is a quality flag, not a guarantee; a pollster's departure from the association is worth noting but is not itself evidence of bias.
  • Published political polls (1News–Verian, RNZ–Reid Research, Taxpayers' Union–Curia, Roy Morgan, Talbot Mills, others): Party-vote intention and preferred prime minister over time. Cite each poll individually with fieldwork dates, sample size and sponsor. Sponsor is a disclosure requirement and a bias flag, not a reason to exclude.
  • Wikipedia: Opinion polling for the 2026 New Zealand general election: Running table of published polls with fieldwork dates, sample sizes and a link to each source. The most complete public index of published polls. Use it to find polls, then cite the poll itself. Page title UNVERIFIED.

Considered and excluded

  • Chapel Hill Expert Survey (CHES) (link): Europe-only; New Zealand is not covered. The 2024 wave covers 31 countries: all EU member states except Luxembourg, plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Turkey and the United Kingdom. Independently confirmed by CSES's own party crosswalk, which states that CHES identifiers are unavailable for New Zealand.
  • PopuList: Europe-only (31 countries). New Zealand out of scope.
  • Vote Compass New Zealand: Methodologically serious (TVNZ with Vox Pop Labs and university academics, 182,682 respondents in 2023) but the party-position matrix is not published, and the data was not made publicly available. Reputable but a dead end as a data source.
  • theyworkforyou.co.nz: No live New Zealand equivalent of TheyWorkForYou exists; the domain appears squatted. Returned 403 and could not be independently confirmed. Treat as not-a-source either way.
  • WhichParty NZ: Claims seven parties across 33 questions with written justifications and sources, but the operator is anonymous and the site blocks automated access. Cannot be verified as independent.
  • voted.nz: CC-BY 4.0 and explicitly non-partisan, but covers only the 50th-52nd Parliaments and explicitly excludes party votes. Effectively stale; no API.
  • Knowledge Basket: Holds Hansard from September 1987 but is commercial and subscription-gated.
  • Figure.NZ: Live and non-advocacy, but a re-presenter of other agencies' data. Cite the upstream source instead.
  • Democracy Project (Bryce Edwards), PublicData NZ, MyElectorate, PartyMap: Advocacy or commercial aggregators. Excluded by the neutrality standard.
  • historical-new-zealand-hansard.com, opengovernment.org.nz, open.nz, nzdotstat.stats.govt.nz: Dead. DNS failure or decommissioned.
